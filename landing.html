Answers

Students get the answer. You get the source.

Passages are pulled out of your own documents before a word is written, so the reply is your material in the student’s language. Students see a clean answer with no file names; you can open the passages behind any reply.

Five passages per answer by default, and weak matches are dropped before the model sees them.

What’s the difference between metaphor and simile?

A simile says one thing is like another and keeps the comparison visible. A metaphor says it simply is, and asks you to accept the substitution — what your handout calls the difference between comparing and claiming.

Specifications

Accounts
Email and password, with roles held server-side. Teacher registration is gated by a signup code kept as a platform secret.
Material accepted
PDFs, Word files, and typed rules. Uploads are split and indexed in batches; the default ceiling is 10 MB per file.
Class isolation
Each class is indexed into its own namespace. A query against one class cannot reach another class’s documents.
Records
Conversations are stored per student account and scoped to the class they began in. Analytics are visible to the owning teacher only.
Retrieval
Five passages per answer by default, discarded below a similarity floor. Questions that clear no passages are recorded as gaps.
Hosting
Managed cloud infrastructure, built from the repository on push. Secrets are injected by the platform; request rate is throttled per account.

What we do

Chronos gives a school an AI tutor it can actually account for. A teacher uploads the documents for a class; students in that class ask questions and get answers drawn from those documents alone. Nothing else is consulted, no other class’s material is reachable, and anything the material does not cover is returned to the teacher as a gap rather than answered from guesswork.
